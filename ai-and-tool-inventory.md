# AI & Tool Inventory

*Every tool, one page — including what the AI can see.*

You can't govern what you can't list. This is the working table: every tool and subscription, the job it does, who owns it, what it costs, and whether its AI features are switched on. Thirty minutes, honestly spent, and the whole stack is visible for the first time.

**Time:** ~30 minutes · Works offline · No email required

## How to use this

Set aside thirty minutes. Open your card and bank statements, your phone's subscriptions screen, and your inbox's "receipt" search, then list every tool the business pays for or relies on, one row each. Don't tidy as you go — a complete messy list beats a neat partial one.

The two example rows below show the level of detail that works: plain words, one line per tool. For "AI features on?" use Yes / No / Unsure — **Unsure is a legitimate answer**; every Unsure is a question worth asking this month.

## When the table is full, look for four things

1. **Two tools doing one job.** Same column-two answer twice? That's a decision waiting — keep one, or write down why you keep both.
2. **Blank owners.** A tool nobody owns is a subscription nobody will ever cancel and a setting nobody will ever check. Blanks are findings, not failures.
3. **The Unsure entries.** Each one is a fifteen-minute settings check: does this tool have AI features on, and what can they read?
4. **The total.** Add the cost column. For most small businesses the number is a surprise, and the strongest argument for a quarterly re-read of this table.
