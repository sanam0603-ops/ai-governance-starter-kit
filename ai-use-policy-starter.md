# AI Use Policy Starter

*A one-page AI policy your team will actually read.*

Five short sections: why, which tools, what never goes in, when a human reviews, and where exceptions get written down. Fill in the blanks, adapt the words, and you have a working policy today — improvable forever.

**Time:** ~20 minutes · Works offline · No email required

## Before you start

This is a starting policy, not a legal document — one page your team can actually read, covering the five decisions that matter. Fill the blanks, delete what doesn't apply, and resist the urge to make it longer: a policy that fits on one page is a policy that gets followed.

> AI use at **[your business]** · Effective **[date]** · Owned by **[name]** · Next review **[date]**

## 1 · Why this exists

We use AI tools to work faster and think better. This page keeps that safe: it says which tools we use, what never goes into them, and when a person checks the output. It protects our clients' trust and our own name.

## 2 · The tools we use

We use the tools on our inventory, nothing else for work. **[Name]** approves new tools; approval takes one message and adds one line to the inventory.

> Pair this with the **AI & Tool Inventory** — the policy points at the inventory instead of listing tools that will be stale in a month.

## 3 · What never goes in

Client names tied to their situations. Passwords and keys. Financial account details. Other people's personal information. Anything under NDA. When unsure, we ask first — asking is free.

> Edit this to match your own never-enter list from the **Safe Input Guide**.

## 4 · When a person reviews AI work

Anything AI helped produce that reaches a client, a contract, a price, or the public is read by a person who understands the work before it goes out. No exceptions when we're busy — busy is when it matters.

## 5 · Exceptions and open questions

Exceptions are allowed — and written down in our decision log the same day, with who approved them. An exception that isn't written down didn't happen.

> Acknowledged by: **[name]**

## Keeping it alive

- **Put the review date in the calendar now.** A policy without a re-read date is a screenshot, not a rule.
- **Read it aloud once with the team.** Two minutes. The questions people ask are the policy working.
- **Change it when it's wrong.** If an exception keeps recurring, the exception is the real rule — write it in.
